# DeepVerify Confidence & Explainability Rules

## 1. Confidence ≠ Truth
- Model confidence scores reflect statistical probabilities (the model's internal certainty), NOT factual, absolute truth.
- The UI MUST NEVER definitively state "This video is FAKE" without proper calibration and disclaimers.
- Prefer phrasing like: "Model output: 92% Fake, 8% Real."
- The UI must clearly explain to the user what the confidence score actually means.

## 2. Explainability & Grad-CAM Claims
- Grad-CAM heatmaps serve as visual evidence of where the model's representation was most highly activated.
- Grad-CAM MUST NOT be presented as absolute causal proof that a specific region is the "fake" part of the image. It is an interpretability tool, not a ground-truth localization.

## 3. Acknowledging Limitations
- The system and its documentation must acknowledge limitations honestly.
- Cross-generator generalization (testing on fakes generated by a model not seen in training) should be presented as an experimental boundary, not a universal guarantee.
- Do not make universal claims about the system's ability to detect all forms of manipulation.
